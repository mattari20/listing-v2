# Institution Comparison, Ratings, Reviews and Trust

## Comparison
Allow students to compare a small, usable shortlist (target 3–5; confirm in usability research) across:
- Distance and location.
- Institution type/sector and education level.
- Exact programme and campus availability.
- Eligibility, duration and admission status.
- Published fees with period and effective date.
- Recognition/affiliation verification.
- Facilities, transport, hostel and scholarships.
- Review dimensions and review count.

Comparison must show missing or stale values instead of treating them as zero. Ranking and scoring methodology must be explainable; no paid result may be presented as organic.

## Student ratings
Candidate dimensions: teaching quality, faculty, facilities, administration, fee value, learning environment and student support. The final rubric, scale, weighting (if any) and anti-manipulation design require M1 research and an ADR. Do not assume a composite score is weighted or statistically representative without a documented method.

## Reviews and responses
- Written reviews and structured ratings.
- Helpful feedback and report/appeal mechanisms.
- Institution right of reply without unilateral deletion power.
- Moderation for spam, threats, harassment, personal data, defamation risk and conflicts of interest.
- Clear community guidelines and transparent moderation reasons.
- Fraud detection and duplicate/spam controls with human appeal.

## Verified experience
Do not label a reviewer a verified student without a proportionate, privacy-preserving verification process and evidence. Avoid exposing enrollment identifiers or sensitive documents publicly. Consider alternative labels for unverified community feedback.

## Minors and privacy
Because many users may be children, age-appropriate design, consent, data minimization, safeguarding, retention limits and jurisdiction-specific legal review are mandatory before collecting personal information or publishing user-generated content.
