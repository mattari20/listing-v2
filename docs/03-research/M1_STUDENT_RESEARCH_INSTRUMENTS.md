# M1-S01 — Adult Participant Interview and Survey Kit

**Status:** Draft research instruments; not approved for participant recruitment or distribution.  
**Intended participants:** Adults aged 18 or older who have recently made an education choice, plus adult parents/guardians who supported such a choice.  
**Use:** Research preparation only. Do not publish as a live survey or contact participants until the owner approves the recruitment channel, consent process and data-retention plan.

## 1. Research safeguards

- Begin with adults only. Do not recruit minors under this draft.
- Participation must be voluntary; explain purpose, duration, recording, data use and withdrawal.
- Do not ask for CNIC, B-Form, roll number, exact marks, phone number, email, home address, institution login or copies of documents.
- Avoid collecting exact household income or other unnecessary sensitive financial details. Use broad affordability bands only if needed and approved.
- Do not record audio/video by default. If recording is proposed, obtain separate explicit consent.
- Use participant codes such as ADULT-STU-001; keep scheduling/contact information outside research notes.
- Do not publish identifiable quotes. Paraphrase or use consented, anonymized quotations.
- Define retention and deletion dates before fieldwork.
- Stop the session if a participant begins sharing sensitive identifiers; do not transcribe or retain them.

## 2. Draft invitation

You are invited to take part in a voluntary research interview about how adults in Pakistan find and choose educational institutions, programmes or training options. The purpose is to understand the information people need and the difficulties they face when comparing options and applying.

The interview should take approximately 30–40 minutes. You do not need to share your name, identification number, marksheet, exact income or other private documents. You may skip any question or stop at any time. Your answers will be summarized without identifying you and used only for product research.

This invitation is a draft. It must not be sent until the owner approves the recruitment channel and research handling arrangements.

## 3. Draft consent script

Before starting, the researcher should explain:
1. The research is for understanding education-search and decision journeys.
2. Participation is voluntary and unrelated to admission, grades or services.
3. The participant may skip questions or withdraw without giving a reason.
4. No government ID, marksheet or exact financial information is required.
5. Notes will be anonymized; any recording requires separate consent.
6. The proposed retention/deletion arrangements and how to contact the research owner.

**Consent confirmation (researcher records yes/no, not a signature or ID):**
- I am 18 or older.
- I understand the purpose and voluntary nature of this interview.
- I agree to anonymized notes being used for product research.
- Separate optional recording consent: Yes / No.

If age or consent is uncertain, do not proceed.

## 4. Interview guide — adult students/recent applicants

### A. Context and transition
1. Think about the most recent time you had to choose a school, college, university, programme or training course. What triggered the decision?
2. What education stage or qualification had you completed at that point? A broad category is enough.
3. When did you start looking, and what deadline or event shaped your timing?
4. Who helped or influenced the decision?

### B. Discovery
5. Where did you first look for options (people, websites, social media, official portals, school/college, counsellor or another source)?
6. Which sources did you trust most, and why?
7. What information was difficult to find or verify?
8. Did you search by location, programme, qualification, institution name or another way?

### C. Evaluation and comparison
9. What were the three most important factors in your decision?
10. How did you compare programme fit, eligibility, recognition/affiliation, fees, transport/distance, schedule and facilities?
11. What costs or requirements surprised you after shortlisting?
12. What made you reject an option?
13. Were there options you could not consider because of cost, distance, timing, entry requirements or other constraints? Broad descriptions are sufficient.

### D. Application and outcome
14. What steps did you take after choosing an option?
15. Which application, document, board, equivalence or deadline step was confusing?
16. Where did you get authoritative confirmation?
17. What would have helped you avoid delay, confusion or a poor choice?

### E. Reflection
18. If you could have had one reliable page comparing your options, what information would it need?
19. What would make you distrust such a page?
20. What information should never be public?

## 5. Interview guide — adult parents/guardians

1. What education decision did you recently help a learner make?
2. What role did you play, and what role did the learner play?
3. Where did you search for institutions or programmes?
4. What made an institution appear credible or questionable?
5. How did you evaluate location, travel, safety, schedule, affordability and programme suitability?
6. What admission or eligibility information was difficult to understand?
7. What did you need to verify directly with the institution?
8. Which decision factors differed between you and the learner?
9. What would a useful comparison service need to show?
10. What information about the learner should remain private?

## 6. Neutral follow-up prompts

Use only when needed:
- “Can you walk me through what happened next?”
- “What made that information convincing?”
- “How did you verify it?”
- “What did you do when the information was missing?”
- “Could you give an example without naming people or sharing private documents?”

Do not lead participants toward a preferred feature or answer.

## 7. Draft short survey

This survey is for adult respondents only. It should be deployed only after interview findings refine the wording and response options.

1. **Which best describes your most recent education decision?** School / College or intermediate / University or degree / Technical or vocational training / Postgraduate / Other.
2. **Who was the decision mainly for?** Myself / My child or dependent / Someone I supported.
3. **Where did you first search?** Search engine / Official institution website / Government or regulator portal / Social media / Family or friends / Teacher or counsellor / Other.
4. **Which information was hardest to find?** Eligibility / Programme details / Recognition or affiliation / Fees and additional costs / Admission dates / Scholarships / Location and transport / Facilities / Other.
5. **Which factors mattered most?** Select up to five from programme fit, recognition, total cost, distance/travel, schedule, reputation, faculty, facilities, safety/accessibility, scholarships and other.
6. **How easy was it to compare options?** Very easy / Somewhat easy / Neither / Somewhat difficult / Very difficult.
7. **Did you find conflicting or outdated information?** Yes / No / Not sure.
8. **What did you do to verify important information?** Contacted institution / Checked official regulator or board / Asked teacher/counsellor / Asked family/friends / Relied on published information / Other.
9. **What device did you mainly use?** Smartphone / Computer / Shared device / Other.
10. **What one improvement would have helped most?** Optional free text; remind respondents not to include identifying or sensitive information.

Do not collect name, phone, email, ID, exact location or institution application number in this survey draft.

## 8. Proposed sampling and analysis

- Proposed initial qualitative planning range: 12–15 adult interviews, adjusted after recruitment feasibility and evidence saturation review. This is a planning proposal, not a claim of statistical representativeness or a fixed commitment.
- Seek variation across recent SSC-to-HSSC, HSSC-to-tertiary/skills, current tertiary, vocational and parent/guardian experiences.
- Record participant type, broad transition category and broad region only where necessary and consented.
- Use a consistent coding framework: trigger, stage, actors, search channels, decision factors, evidence/trust, cost/access, application friction, outcome and unmet need.
- Compare patterns across participant groups; retain contradictory cases rather than averaging them away.
- Survey results, if collected, should be reported with recruitment method, response count, missingness and selection limitations.

## 9. Owner approval required before fieldwork

The following must be decided before distribution:
- Recruitment channels and who will invite participants.
- Whether any incentives are offered and the budget, if any.
- Research owner/contact and consent/withdrawal process.
- Note storage, access permissions, retention and deletion schedule.
- Whether recordings are necessary (default: no).
- Whether and how minors may be included later under a separate approved safeguarding protocol.

Until these decisions are approved, this document remains a preparation kit only.
