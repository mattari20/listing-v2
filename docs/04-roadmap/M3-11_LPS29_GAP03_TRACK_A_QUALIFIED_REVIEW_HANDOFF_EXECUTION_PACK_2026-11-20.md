## 6. Mandatory Track A questions

The written assessment must address each item and identify missing facts rather than assume answers:

1. **Applicability:** Which laws, duties and professional standards apply to the actual operator and the exact private synthetic-only activity? Which facts are unknown?
2. **Identifiability:** Could account identifiers, staff/reviewer information, audit metadata, logs, cookies, telemetry or other artifacts identify or relate to a natural person despite synthetic business fixtures?
3. **Roles:** Who is the accountable operator/controller or equivalent decision-maker? Are processors or subprocessors involved? Which conclusions cannot be made before an actual environment exists?
4. **Purpose and minimization:** Are the stated purposes, fields, access boundaries and synthetic-only constraints adequate for the exact activity?
5. **Lifecycle:** Are retention, deletion, audit, session, log, backup, export and propagation controls sufficiently defined? Identify where repository evidence is incomplete and what must be decided before a later stage.
6. **Safeguarding:** Does the expressly child/student/guardian-excluded scope create any remaining safeguarding obligations or foreseeable exposure that needs a condition? Identify triggers requiring a new review if scope changes.
7. **Security and confidentiality:** What minimum organizational, access, confidentiality and security conditions apply before any reviewer receives materials or any later environment is proposed?
8. **Formal assessment:** Is a formal privacy impact assessment, data protection impact assessment, safeguarding assessment, additional legal opinion or other review required for this exact scope?
9. **Limitations:** What cannot be concluded without target hosting region, effective runtime configuration, processor contracts, access roster, log destinations or other presently unavailable facts?
10. **Conclusion:** Is the exact Track A scope APPROVE, APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS, DO NOT APPROVE or UNABLE TO CONCLUDE? List non-waivable blockers and objective evidence needed to close each condition.

The reviewer must distinguish current-scope conclusions from recommendations for a future hosted or source-bearing product.

## 7. Required written report structure

The report must contain:
- reviewer identity, role, qualifications, relevant jurisdiction/specialism and independence/conflict declaration;
- exact Track A scope, purpose, audience, exclusions and reviewed repository commit;
- documents/evidence actually examined and method used;
- assumptions, missing facts, evidence not available and limitations;
- reasoned findings with severity/priority and affected scope;
- required remediation or conditions, accountable role and objective closure evidence;
- non-waivable blockers before any next stage;
- explicit overall conclusion: **APPROVE**, **APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS**, **DO NOT APPROVE** or **UNABLE TO CONCLUDE**;
- reassessment triggers, report date, validity/expiry assumptions and verifiable professional issuance.

A verbal discussion, unsigned note, generic statement of comfort, blank template or receipt acknowledgement is not a qualified report.

## 8. Post-report decision protocol

1. Mark the report **RECEIVED — NOT YET VALIDATED**. Receipt is not acceptance.
2. Validate reviewer identity, competence, jurisdiction, independence, conflict declaration, scope, date and evidence list.
3. Check that the report answers the mandatory questions and states limitations and a clear conclusion.
4. Register each finding/condition with an ID, severity, scope, accountable role, due date and objective closure evidence.
5. Keep confidential report contents in an approved restricted location. Record only a non-sensitive reference and disposition in the repository.
6. The Product Owner records a separate disposition: ACCEPT, ACCEPT WITH CONDITIONS, REJECT or RETURN FOR CLARIFICATION. Professional findings remain attributable to the reviewer; owner decisions remain attributable to the Product Owner.
7. If conditions require code or documentation changes, define a separate bounded synthetic/local/CI agenda. If conditions require hosted access, real data, source activity, external services, participant activity, procurement or spend, stop and obtain a separate exact-scope authorization first.
8. Reassess any material change under LPS-13 C0–C3 change-impact governance.
