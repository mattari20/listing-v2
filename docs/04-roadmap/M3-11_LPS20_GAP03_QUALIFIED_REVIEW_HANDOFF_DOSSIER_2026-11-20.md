## 2. Two distinct review tracks — do not conflate

| Track | Question | Scope now | What it can decide | What it cannot decide |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A — M3-11-02 current private-scope legal/privacy/safeguarding review** | Is the exact Option A / Option 1 private V2 synthetic-only acceptance scope acceptable, and what conditions apply before provisioning/access? | Existing private product scope, repository evidence, synthetic fixtures, proposed but unprovisioned private environment; no real subjects/data | Applicability, operator/controller roles, synthetic-scope obligations, security/privacy conditions, required further assessments and blockers for the exact scope | Hosted isolation, actual runtime configuration, real-source permission, future public/child-facing use, production/release approval |
| **B — GAP-03 source-specific rights/privacy/safeguarding review** | May a precisely identified source/material be used for a precisely identified purpose/method/audience/territory/storage/retention/disclosure? | Source taxonomy and empty source-specific assessment template only; no source accessed or selected | A qualified opinion on exact source and exact proposed use after sufficient evidence is supplied | Blanket approval of all sources, permission inferred from public visibility, unexamined sources, or live collection under this agenda |

Track A and Track B require separate written scopes and dispositions. A Track A opinion for synthetic-only private acceptance does not approve any source. A Track B source-specific opinion does not approve the whole service, real-data processing, public launch, hosted operation or child-directed activity. If a reviewer combines tracks, the report must identify each distinct question and issue a separate conclusion for each.

## 4. Reviewer workstream and competence matrix

These are required competence profiles, not named people or appointments. The Product Owner must separately decide whether a given workstream is necessary for the exact scope and approve the reviewer/engagement before any handoff or expenditure.

| Workstream | Required competence | Minimum independence/qualification evidence | Required written output |
|---|---|---|---|
| Legal applicability / Pakistan law | Practising or otherwise demonstrably qualified legal professional with relevant technology, privacy, IP or education-sector competence in applicable jurisdiction(s) | Qualification, relevant experience, jurisdictional competence, conflict declaration and scoped engagement | Applicable-law analysis, assumptions, limitations, obligations, blockers and conditions |
| Copyright / database / contract / platform rights | Qualified IP/technology counsel or rights specialist with database, licensing, platform terms and commercial reuse experience | Relevant experience and source/use-specific independence | Source-by-source rights matrix, permitted/prohibited acts, attribution, extraction, caching, derivative, redistribution, expiry and withdrawal conditions |
| Privacy / data protection | Qualified privacy professional or legal counsel with relevant data protection and information governance experience | Demonstrated privacy assessment competence and conflict declaration | Exact-scope data inventory/flow review, purposes, data subjects, lawful basis, minimization, retention/deletion, processors/transfers, rights, breach and risk findings |
| Child safeguarding / education data | Qualified safeguarding professional and/or legal/privacy specialist experienced in children/minors and education contexts | Demonstrated safeguarding competence, jurisdictional relevance and independence | Current-scope exclusion assessment and explicit triggers/conditions for any future child/minor/guardian proposal; no child activity authorized |
| Research / ethics (conditional) | Competent research ethics reviewer where human-participant research is ever proposed | Relevant institutional/ethics authority and independence | Applicability determination and required ethics review; no participant research authorized |
| Source-specific technical/licensing specialist (conditional) | Competent specialist for a specific dataset/platform/geospatial or technical license when needed | Relevant source-specific experience | Exact technical/contractual restrictions and evidence limitations; cannot substitute for legal advice where required |

A reviewer may cover multiple domains only where their qualifications genuinely cover each domain and the report states the basis. A single generic “legal review” label is insufficient where distinct privacy, IP/database, safeguarding or research-ethics competence is needed.

## 6. Mandatory reviewer questions and deliverables

### 6.1 Track A — current private synthetic acceptance

The reviewer should explicitly answer:
- What laws, duties and professional standards apply to the actual operator and exact private synthetic-only activity? What facts are missing?
- Does the synthetic-only scope still involve personal data, account identifiers, staff/reviewer information, logs, telemetry, cookies or other identifiable information?
- Who is the accountable operator/controller or equivalent decision-maker, and are any processors involved? Which facts remain unknown because the host/environment is not provisioned?
- Are purposes, data minimization, access, retention, deletion, backups, logs, sessions and audit records sufficiently bounded for the exact proposed private activity?
- What security, privacy, contractual or organizational conditions must be satisfied before provisioning or any internal reviewer access?
- Does any proposed behavior exceed Option A / Option 1? If so, identify it as a blocker rather than silently expanding scope.
- Is a formal PIA/DPIA, additional legal opinion, safeguarding review or other assessment required before the exact private activity?
- What cannot be concluded without actual host, region, runtime, processor or environment evidence?

### 6.2 Track B — source-specific rights/privacy/safeguarding

For each source and use, the reviewer should explicitly answer:
- Who owns or controls the material, and what authority supports that conclusion?
- Which rights attach to facts, wording, databases, photographs, logos, maps, documents, metadata and other assets?
- Which exact acts are permitted: access, retrieval, extraction, storage, transformation, caching, attribution, linking, display, redistribution, commercial use and derivative use?
- What terms, license version, contractual or platform restrictions apply? What is the evidence date and expiry/reassessment trigger?
- What privacy basis and data-subject protections apply independently of copyright/license permission?
- Are children/minors, education records, staff contacts, sensitive affiliations or other higher-risk data present or inferable?
- What are the required security, processor, cross-border, retention, deletion, withdrawal and correction controls?
- What exact limitations, prohibited uses and stop conditions must be encoded in the source-specific decision?

### 6.3 Required report format

Each written assessment must include:
- reviewer name/organization, role, qualification, jurisdiction/specialism and independence/conflict declaration;
- exact track, scope, purpose, audience, source/candidate commit, documents and evidence reviewed;
- assumptions, missing facts, evidence unavailable, methods and limitations;
- applicable obligations and reasoned findings;
- severity/priority, required remediation, accountable role and objective closure evidence;
- non-waivable blockers and conditions before the next permitted stage;
- explicit conclusion: APPROVE, APPROVE WITH CONDITIONS, DO NOT APPROVE or UNABLE TO CONCLUDE;
- expiry/reassessment triggers and date;
- signature or otherwise verifiable professional issuance.

## 7. Reviewer response and Product Owner disposition protocol

The existing `M3-11-02_REVIEWER_RESPONSE_TEMPLATE_2026-11-17.md` remains the response form. LPS-20 clarifies its use:

1. Keep one separate response per reviewer/workstream and per distinct track.
2. Verify qualification, jurisdiction, independence and conflict declarations before relying on the report.
3. Confirm the exact source commit, review scope and documents actually examined.
4. Classify the submission as RECEIVED, SCOPE-VALIDATED, REVIEWED, CONDITIONAL, BLOCKED, REJECTED or UNABLE-TO-CONCLUDE; receipt alone is not acceptance.
5. Register each finding with severity, affected scope, remediation, owner role, due date and objective closure evidence.
6. Separate professional conclusions from Product Owner decisions. The reviewer provides professional findings; the Product Owner decides whether to accept the proposed scope/conditions.
7. Do not treat ChatGPT, CI, repository authorship or the internal owner review as the qualified reviewer.
8. Store confidential reports in an approved restricted location; keep only a non-sensitive reference, status and disposition in the repository.
9. Any scope, source, field, audience, method, processor, data subject or hosting change requires reassessment under LPS-13.
10. A new exact-scope authorization is required before any activity that crosses the current boundary.
